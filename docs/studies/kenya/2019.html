2019 Tusome

From 2015 to 2022, Tusome, a USAID-funded project, aimed to improve foundational literacy skills among Grade 1, 2, and 3 learners across more than 20,000 schools in Kenya. Data were collected using the Early Grade Reading Assessment (EGRA) in English and Kiswahili across a pilot impact evaluation, baseline, midline, and endline in 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, and 2019. In 2021, additional EGRA data were collected to understand the impacts of COVID-19 on learning outcomes, and in 2022, further EGRA data were collected to evaluate a supplemental pilot remedial program addressing learning losses from school closures during the pandemic. The project was nationally representative.

Sampling Description

The Tusome evaluation employed a three-stage cluster sampling design and was designed to produce nationally representative estimates of early grade reading outcomes for learners in public and Alternative Provision of Basic Education and Training (APBET) schools in Kenya. In the first stage, 26 of Kenya's 47 counties, spanning all eight former provinces, were selected. In the second stage, 204 schools were selected proportionally within sampled counties using independent sampling frames for public and APBET schools. The same schools were followed across the baseline, midline, and endline surveys, while a new sample of pupils was selected at each survey round. In the third stage, 24 pupils per school were selected for assessment, comprising 12 learners from Class 1 and 12 learners from Class 2, with equal numbers of boys and girls in each class. The harmonized dataset includes 4,866 pupils from the 2015 baseline, 4,671 pupils from the 2016 midline, and 4,850 pupils from the 2019 endline. Survey estimates are representative of early grade learners in eligible public and APBET schools in Kenya. Users should verify the appropriate survey weights (`wt_final`) and survey design variables against the original Tusome evaluation documentation before producing published estimates. The harmonized `school_id` is not comparable across the 2015–2019 survey waves and should not be used to identify schools longitudinally.
