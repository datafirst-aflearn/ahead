2008 EGRA Plus

From 2008 to 2010, EGRA Plus—a project funded by USAID and the World Bank, and implemented by RTI International in collaboration with the Liberian Ministry of Education—aimed to evaluate early grade literacy interventions and track progress toward quality improvements in early grade reading instruction. Globally representative across 176 public and community primary schools, data were collected using the Early Grade Reading Assessment (EGRA) in English across baseline, midline, and endline rounds. Methodologically, the study was implemented as a cluster randomized controlled trial (RCT) utilizing a school-level panel design that tracked the same institutional units over time, paired with repeated cross-sectional student assessments administered to randomly selected cohorts of Grade 2 and Grade 3 learners. To measure empirical impact, schools were randomly assigned to one of three arms:Full Treatment Group: Received intensive phonics-based teacher training, pedagogical support, and reading books.Light Treatment Group: Received an information-only intervention focused on school accountability and student assessment tools.Control Group: Continued with standard, unaltered national instruction.Ultimately, this multi-arm design captured both longitudinal institutional shifts and immediate literacy gains across different intervention types within the public education system

Assessment Subtasks

The subtasks included in this assessment survey are described below.

Letter Name Knowledge

The letter name knowledge subtask measures a student's ability to identify letter names. Students are shown letters and asked to name them.

Addition Level 1

Addition level 1 measures a student's ability to solve basic addition problems, usually with small numbers appropriate to the grade. The score is the number of problems answered correctly.

Addition Level 2

Addition level 2 measures a student's ability to solve more difficult addition problems than level 1, often involving larger numbers or additional steps. The score is the number of problems answered correctly.

Subtraction Level 1

Subtraction level 1 measures a student's ability to solve basic subtraction problems. The score is the number of problems answered correctly.

Subtraction Level 2

Subtraction level 2 measures a student's ability to solve more difficult subtraction problems than level 1. The score is the number of problems answered correctly.

Number Identification

The number identification subtask measures whether a student can recognise and name or select numbers. Students are presented with numbers and asked to identify them. The score is typically the number of items answered correctly.

Number Discrimination

The number discrimination subtask measures a student's ability to compare quantities or identify which of two or more numbers or sets is larger, smaller, or the same. It assesses early numeracy and magnitude understanding.

Word Problems

The word problems subtask measures a student's ability to solve short mathematics story problems. Students must understand the problem, choose the correct operation, and find the answer. The score is the number of problems answered correctly.

Nonwords

The nonword subtask tests students' skill in using letter-sound connections to figure out ("decode") words. While many students learn to memorize a broad range of "sight" words, they need skills to decode less-familiar words. In this subtask, students were given a list of made-up words that do not exist in the language tested but follow a typical spelling/sound combination of the language.

Oral Reading Fluency (ORF)

The oral reading fluency (ORF) subtask measures how quickly and accurately a student can read. It is a core component of EGRA because it brings together lower-level reading skills (such as decoding and familiar word recognition) with how quickly and easily the student can read a given word (called automaticity). Students were given a short, written passage on a topic that was familiar to them. They were asked to read it out loud "quickly but carefully." The score is reported as correct words per minute (cwpm).

Reading Comprehension

Comprehension is the main goal of reading—understanding what is read. Comprehension is a complex task that requires some ability in all other reading skills. This subtask is paired with the ORF subtask. Depending on how much of the ORF passage the student was able to read, the assessor asked the student questions about the story. The score is the number of questions answered correctly.

Familiar Words

The familiar word reading subtask is similar in format to the nonword reading subtask except that it presents the student with words they are expected to be able to read at their grade level and have likely encountered before. Students were instructed to read aloud as many words as they could in one minute.

Different Initial Sound

This subtask measures whether a student can identify words that begin with a different sound from a reference word. The score is the number of items answered correctly.

Sampling

Two-stage cluster design: schools → pupils. Schools were clustered within districts for the purposes of treatment randomisation, with nearby schools grouped into geographic clusters. Stage 1: 176 schools achieved at baseline (target: 180), drawn from a nationally representative frame of Liberian public and community primary schools; schools were randomly assigned at the cluster level to one of three arms — Full Treatment (59 schools), Light Treatment (60 schools), or Control (57 schools). The same schools were followed across all three rounds (school-level panel); a new pupil sample was drawn independently in each round. Stage 2: 10 or 20 pupils per school assessed depending on school size and teacher count, targeting a minimum of 10 pupils from Grade 2 and 10 from Grade 3, selected by assessors using systematic random sampling from pupils present on the assessment day. Harmonized pool waves: 2008 baseline (2,988 pupils; Grades 2–3), 2009 midline (2,882), 2010 endline (2,688). Designed to support impact estimation within a cluster RCT rather than population-representative inference; no sampling weights are described in the evaluation report
